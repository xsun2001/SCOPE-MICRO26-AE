# Evaluation for `figure_paper_requested_e2e_throughput_with_speedup`

## What It Shows

Figure X is a 4x6 composite comparison of end-to-end attention efficiency across six representative devices: H100, B200, AWSv3, AWSv4, TPUv3, and TPUv6e. The first and third rows plot effective attention throughput for FP16 and INT8, respectively, across prefill lengths from 2K to 32K; the second and fourth rows directly below them show the corresponding `SCNA / FlashAttention` speedup bars. The legend is: `Baseline` (maroon dashed), `FlashAttention` (teal solid), and `SCNA` (navy solid, our design). The throughput metric is not raw hardware FLOP/s; it is the useful attention GEMM throughput, computed as `(QK FLOPs + AV FLOPs) / total latency`, intentionally excluding softmax/exp work. As noted in the figure metadata, all runs disable HBM throttling, and the GPU suite also disables on-chip I/O throttling, so this figure isolates compute and scheduling behavior rather than bandwidth-limited deployment behavior.

## Key Results

SCNA is the best design in every plotted configuration, and its advantage grows with sequence length. In FP16, SCNA delivers a 1.28x overall geomean speedup over FlashAttention across all plotted device-context points, increasing from 1.04-1.25x at 2K to 1.08-1.70x at 32K. The smallest FP16 gain is on H100, which reaches 1.08x at 32K, while TPUv6e shows the largest FP16 gain at 1.70x. INT8 is much more favorable to SCNA: the overall geomean speedup rises to 1.80x, and the 32K speedups range from 1.32x on TPUv3 to 3.05x on B200. At the longest context, SCNA reaches 870 TFLOPS effective throughput on H100 FP16 and 2,044 TFLOPS on B200 FP16; in INT8, it reaches 1,740 TFLOPS on H100, 4,078 TFLOPS on B200, 1,221 TFLOPS on AWSv4, and 850 TFLOPS on TPUv6e. The lower speedup bars are nearly monotonic with context length on all devices, which shows that SCNA's relative benefit strengthens exactly in the long-context regime.

## Analysis and Insights

The underlying latency breakdown explains the trend. In the Baseline and FlashAttention designs, long-context execution is still dominated by softmax or the surrounding matmul pair, whereas SCNA removes the standalone softmax stage and shifts the work into a fused systolic-core execution. At INT8 and 32K, FlashAttention spends 13.1 ms in softmax on B200, 35.9 ms on AWSv4, and 57.8 ms on TPUv6e, while SCNA reduces total execution to 4.3 ms, 14.4 ms, and 20.7 ms, respectively. On AWSv4, SCNA is no longer compute-limited at 32K; it becomes on-chip-I/O-limited, which is a strong sign that the nonlinear compute bottleneck has largely been eliminated. The cross-device ranking also matches the architectural motivation: devices with weaker vector-side support relative to tensor throughput benefit more from repurposing systolic resources for nonlinear attention work. H100 shows the smallest gains, while TPUv6e and B200 see the largest. INT8 gains are further amplified by conversion overheads in conventional flows, but the advantage is not only a conversion artifact: even under matched no-conversion comparisons, SCNA still retains 2.59x speedup on B200 and 1.88x on TPUv6e at 32K. Overall, the figure shows that turning softmax/exp into first-class systolic-array work converts attention from a vector/nonlinearity bottleneck into a fused array-centric computation, and that this benefit becomes most valuable at long context and low precision.
